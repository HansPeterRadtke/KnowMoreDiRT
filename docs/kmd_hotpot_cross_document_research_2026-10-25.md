# KnowMoreDiRT: real cross-document identity and context benchmark — 2026-10-25

This report continues the **original owner's October 17–18 research question**: can a source-scoped knowledge agent reliably combine remote text passages, preserve writer, referent, quotation/dream/conditional/time and negation scope, derive valid new conclusions and submit genuinely new claims to independently discriminating tests? This is **not a demonstration that the deployed KMD agent does so**. The separate scientific-discovery report already covers the experimental Swanson, OPERA, SciFact and ContractNLI evidence; this new study addresses **source-to-source identity links and evidence availability** at materially greater scale than previous hand-authored probes. Earlier KMD three hard actor-identifier failures `hrq086`, `hrq087`, `hrq089` and additional context failure IDs `hrq057`, `hrq104` remain unchanged; so do original gold answers, source text and the initial 12 synthetic DRS-oracle scores.

## Independent source and original third-party evidence

Original [HotpotQA dataset and release](https://hotpotqa.github.io/) (Yang, Qi, Zhang et al., [EMNLP 2018 DOI 10.18653/v1/D18-1259](https://aclanthology.org/D18-1259/)), freely distributed under CC BY-SA 4.0. The benchmark explicitly collects natural multi-hop questions with **human-labeled document-title and sentence-index supporting facts** from Wikipedia, two original gold evidence-bearing articles per distractor question. The untouched official `hotpot_dev_distractor_v1.json` was acquired from the public RAGLAB mirror that has the **same SHA-256 as the original publisher's original declared download checksum**: `4e9ecb5c8d3b719f624d66b60f8d56bf227f03914f5f0753d6fa1b359d7104ea`, size **46,320,117 bytes**. Source is held read-only on Raspi at `/data/tmp/kmd-hotpot-original-distractor-20261025.json` and is **not recopied into the research Git**. The original official data were collected against a historical processed Wikipedia snapshot; current live Wikipedia edits must not be allowed to leak into inference. Each original record carries source article title, exact sentence sequence, original question, gold answer and human supporting sentence IDs.

There are **7,405** development questions, all labeled hard, of which **5,918 are bridge** questions where a source relation connects two articles, and **1,487 comparison** questions involving two subjects. **7,345 examples have 10 candidate articles and 60 have fewer**, so *this evaluates retrieval inside provided candidate documents*, not live internet-wide retrieval. Every question has two distinct gold supporting article titles in those candidates. Source identity is the original article title, not an unvalidated named-entity cluster inferred from embeddings.

## One frozen untuned full-corpus retrieval experiment

The complete method and source ranking are in `research/devtests_drt_reference/hotpot_multidoc_20261025/predict_unlabelled_retrieval.py` and its **SHA-256-frozen predictions** `frozen_unlabelled_retrieval_20261025.json` (SHA-256 `3b458f9dca38f0fd6a00896928d7df98c6269297c0da4a65fd4b22dfbd76cdf8`). Critically, the predictor accesses **only original question text, candidate article titles, candidate article sentences and stable question ID**; it does **not** inspect gold supporting articles, gold sentence indices, gold answers or type labels. The independent scoring phase `score_external_multidoc_gold.py` loads original human gold *only after predictions were saved*. No ranking parameters were tuned against that dev gold.

The four predeclared methods are lexical BM25 over titles only; BM25 over full title+paragraph; BM25 over title+first two sentences; and **a two-stage title-link candidate procedure**. The last method first selects the best fulltext BM25 article; it then boosts another candidate article only when the first source paragraph **literally names that article's exact original title**. Its boost has fixed scale `0.65 × first article score`; the primary fulltext ranking still governs other choices. A different title-score tie breaker may affect a small number of ties; the follow-up source-offset audit explicitly measures whether a change was actually supported by a literal source-title mention. This is an **identity-reference candidate graph built from real source text**, not a DRS, causal rule, language-model vote or perfectly manually supplied label.

The fixed retrieval scores below are cases for which **both distinct human supporting articles** appear among the **first two** retrieved candidates (not final answer correctness):

| Human query class | Questions | Title BM25 | Fulltext BM25 | Title-linked second hop |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **All** | 7,405 | 2,215 | 2,729 | 3,211 |
| **Bridge** | 5,918 | 956 | 2,194 | 2,692 |
| **Comparison** | 1,487 | 1,259 | 535 | 519 |

For **bridge** questions, title-linked candidate retrieval improved complete two-source recovery by **498/5,918 = +8.415 percentage points** over the standard fulltext BM25 selection, with paired bootstrap 95% interval **+7.587 to +9.243 percentage points**. Paired case counts were **605 improvements, 107 regressions and 5,206 ties**; exact paired McNemar two-sided probability in this annotated benchmark is about `3.41e-85`. For **comparison** questions the identical link rule reduced complete two-source recall by **16/1,487 = -1.076 percentage points** (paired bootstrap 95% interval **-1.681 to -0.538**), with **1 improvement, 17 regressions and 1,469 ties**. The difference between classes is task-dependent and emerges from an unchanged procedure, not from selecting the better method using labels. Over all 7,405 examples, the method improved by **+6.509 percentage points** (95% paired-bootstrap interval +5.820 to +7.184), with 606 wins and 124 losses. These statistical intervals measure variation across the **fixed published question sample**, not scientific universality; benchmark sharing and linguistic source dependence limit external generalization. Original author labels are evidence retrieval gold rather than verified new world facts.

The **title-only BM25** method performs extremely well for comparisons (**1,259 of 1,487** complete source pairs among first two), much better than full-text BM25 and identity links on this subset; that result alone contradicts a universal recommendation to increase semantic structure for every question. Long document text can *dilute* direct entity matching. Relation/source-link expansion helps bridge questions because literal references point to another source, but a generic question comparing two named entities frequently needs *two direct title matches*, not a third source referred to incidentally by the first.

For **source sentence evidence**, within the first four fulltext-ranked documents, the fixed BM25 three-sentence selector recovered **14,438 of 18,005 original human support sentence occurrences**, while adding a local ±1 sentence neighborhood recovered **14,829**. The number of questions with at least **one supporting sentence from both gold documents** among those candidate/source windows increased **4,664 → 4,776**. These are two different metrics: extra recoverable evidence (recall) versus final question-answer quality. This experiment does not prove that longer contexts improve model interpretation; earlier KMD and SciFact results demonstrate interpretation can fail despite correct evidence.

## Post-hoc cross-document identity source audit, including actual errors

`audit_title_link_identity.py` cross-checks original raw source titles and precise sentence-level **source offsets** for every changed second-source result, preserving representative examples in `title_identity_source_link_audit_20261025.json`. The audit necessarily inspected original human gold **after the first scores were known**; it is a forensic *diagnostic* and not a fresh blinded evaluation or a tuned retrieval method.

Exactly **990 of 7,405** ranked pairs changed second document (910 bridge and 80 comparison). Of the 910 bridge changes, **605 repaired** the two-source set, **107 harmed it**, and **198 did not alter the success of complete source-pair recovery**. In all 910, the newly selected article title *literally appeared in the first source article text*. For comparison questions, **79 of the 80** changed second-source choices also had a literal title mention, but yielded **17 losses and no gains**; the final one case was a title-score tie-break gain with no literal mention. Thus almost all observed changes genuinely came from **verifiable source-text identity links**, not an unexplained system behavior. Document name co-occurrence is nevertheless not proof of the *question's intended relationship*.

Real illustrative **bridge recoveries** include “Babyflot” explicitly mentioning **Aeroflot** in its discussion of the Soviet airline monopoly, “Pluralist school” explicitly naming **Leucippus**, and a racing article identifying **Daniel Ricciardo** as a winner; this permits retrieval of the second gold entity without manually supplying its gold ID. But errors are equally real: an article about Lake Wales Ridge Refuge mentioned **Florida scrub**, prompting a wrong link for a question requiring a Florida city, and a Peter Schmeichel article mentioning an IFFHS award drew the algorithm to an award page rather than the relevant person. Source inclusion does not by itself establish the identity relation that answers the question. Raw title/digest/short source context of these examples is in the audit, not retroactively patched in the retriever.

## What this does and does not establish for original KMD questions

**Supported new local generalizable insight within this corpus:** lexical title-reference **source identity relations** can produce a measurable recovery benefit for real non-adjacent multi-document bridge questions over a plain source paragraph score; the exact same relation heuristic can impair direct comparison questions. This gives a concrete, 7,405-question benchmark reason to prioritize **provenance + source links + selective graph traversal**, not automatically the more complex DRS representation. Source provenance identifies which Wikipedia article and sentence supported the title link; explicit referent equality between source mentions would require further extraction and verification. The printed p-values concern benchmark source retrieval, not the universal truth of an extracted article or physiological scientific novelty.

**Unresolved:** this experiment **does not compare the deployed KMD full DRS parser to the strong neighbor-and-title-link method**, does not infer human gold answers, and does not evaluate dreams, fictional worlds, attributed quotations, superseded laws or independent experimental falsification. The earlier real PMB study demonstrates that published automatic DRS parsing can capture some implicit scoped negation beyond a simple lexical regex, but it is isolated short-sentence linguistics; it cannot establish answer-quality gain from multi-document DRS without paired end-to-end evaluation. The earlier eight-case Qwen model source-scope JSON failures and six-case contract analysis remain negative findings rather than generalized proof against DRT. The original research is also **not a demonstration of genuinely new independent empirical scientific findings**—we already documented hypothesis derivation vs independent scientific test using the historical Raynaud/Swanson and neutrino sources.

To resolve the remaining scientific question, benchmark **a validated, production-relevant DRS extractor with exactly the same candidate documents, source-title identity/reference graph, neighboring context and human supporting-fact gold**. Track separate gold provenance recovery, semantic scope/box correctness, ability to preserve quotations and dream/time modes, downstream answer exact/semantic correctness, unsupported-fact rate and model wall time. Source-independent novel discovery must have **independent external experiment or evaluator**, not just a high multi-hop QA score. Until that matched evaluation exists, the appropriate conclusion is **DRS has a theoretically valuable semantic role but its incremental KMD scientific benefit remains unmeasured**.

## New question: how much identity linking requires more than an exact article title?

A further explicitly **post-hoc**, original-source-hash-checked diagnostic `audit_literal_link_limit.py` examined all **5,918 HotpotQA bridge questions**, linking each first-ranked fulltext BM25 article to the two original human evidence titles. The original first article was gold for **5,165/5,918** questions; it was a *wrong article* for **753**, which no second-hop expansion can fix while retaining that incorrect first article. Among the **5,165** where the first gold source was correct, the **literal exact full title of the other gold article appeared anywhere in the first source article's body in only 1,080** cases. In the other **4,085 cases—79.09%—the second title was not literally present**. This does **not** prove there is a hidden coreferent in every one of those 4,085 cases, nor that the exact title link can be expected to work in those cases: a reference may be a shortened surname, alias, event name, descriptive phrase, relation chain or entirely elsewhere.

When the correct first article **did** explicitly contain the second gold title, linked-secondhop selected the other gold article in **1,008/1,080** cases; it missed **72**. When the correct second gold title was *not* an exact string mention, the method still selected that gold article in **1,684/4,085** cases due to ordinary BM25 similarity, but missed the second article **2,401** times. This is a concrete measured frontier: a deterministic exact-title relation can exploit explicit mentions but **cannot resolve unexpressed / nonliteral document-entity relationships without other evidence**. A validated learned entity-linker, coreference model, relation extractor or DRS discourse analysis could plausibly add value there; **no such model was independently validated in this experiment**, so an additional DRS win is a research hypothesis, not a reported fact. Gold-spanning question/paragraph provenance and source excerpts are retained in `literal_identity_reference_ceiling_20261025.json` without changing ranking outputs or original labels. It is a diagnostic *after* human gold has already been scored, not a new fresh heldout benchmark.

### Dependence control: clustering related questions by first retrieved article

The large HotpotQA sample could overstate significance if the same source article generates many nearly equivalent questions. Without changing the original frozen retrieval, `clustered_source_uncertainty.py` ran a **post-hoc 1,750-resample cluster bootstrap**, resampling all questions sharing the same **first fulltext-retrieved article title** as a unit. In 5,918 bridge questions, there were **5,820 such first-article clusters** (largest size 3); the paired link gain remained **+8.415 percentage points**, with 95% first-article-cluster interval **+7.580 to +9.239 points**. Across all 7,405 questions, **7,216 first-source clusters** gave **+6.509 points**, interval **+5.809 to +7.230**. In comparisons, **1,400 clusters** gave **-1.076 points**, interval **-1.679 to -0.541**. The cluster result is similar to the original case bootstrap because most first-retrieved articles were unique. It cannot guarantee independence of underlying second gold sources, alternate titles, repeated Wikipedia facts or pretraining exposures; it is a robustness check, not an independent experimental replication. The recorded JSON has exact original result hashes, method and explicit limitations.

## Real model-answer outcome, tested after freezing independent source contexts

A separate **case-level first-run** experiment tested whether the above title-link improvements actually yield better **human-gold multi-document answers**, rather than declaring increased retrieval recall to be a finished success. `freeze_source_only_model_qa.py` selected 36 original Hotpot questions by SHA256 of original ID within type and *whether the two fixed retrieval methods selected different article pairs*, using **16 bridge-changed, 8 bridge-same, 8 comparison-changed, 4 comparison-same**. Individual answer gold and original annotated supporting title/sentence IDs were *not* consulted when selecting cases, snippets or generating model responses. Because overall Hotpot retrieval labels had already been examined in the previous experiment, this is a **prospectively frozen case-level diagnostic, not a pristine corpus-level independent holdout**. Source/answer pretraining contamination is also possible for HotpotQA, which has been public since 2018.

The same isolated local `Qwen/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct-GGUF` Q4_K_M revision `91cad51170dc346986eccefdc2dd33a9da36ead9` (model bytes SHA256 `6a1a2eb6d15622bf3c96857206351ba97e1af16c30d7a74ee38970e434e9407e`) received *exactly the same model/system prompt and output budget* under both retrieval methods. The only intervention is whether two question-specific candidate source articles are chosen by plain BM25 or the already-frozen second-hop title-reference approach. Source excerpt selection within each ranked document was identical (fixed question-word Jaccard-ranked sentence references, source sentence 0, and one local neighbor, source excerpt budget 1,300 characters per document). All source title, text, question IDs and source hashes are frozen in `frozen_source_only_model_qa_20261025.json`, SHA256 `8c5af13eb3db9b89923a37a2811082113a7e0d28e6c81921512c479d4977ccc2`. No human gold answer or original human support sentence was passed to the model. One model was loaded on Raspi CPU, two inference threads with lowered priority nice 15, no GPU or production server contention, `n_ctx=2304`, temperature zero, maximum 125 output tokens per call. All **72** calls completed once over about **1,165 seconds** with per-call source IDs, raw JSON, token costs and completion reasons checkpointed; no prompts, cases or failed responses were retried after scoring.

Only after all 72 outputs were saved did `score_model_qa_external_gold.py` compare original human answers and supporting documents, using normalized exact match and token-level F1 alongside independent source-title and exact-quotation checks. Across **36 questions**, both methods produced **10/36 exact answers**. The linked method recovered the two human gold articles among selected documents for **15/36** cases versus **12/36** with fulltext ranking, and included at least one original human evidence sentence in **35/36** cases versus **32/36**, yet exact answers **did not improve**: there were **three paired gains, three paired losses, and thirty ties**, paired exact McNemar **p = 1.0**, paired bootstrap 95% difference interval **-13.89 to +13.89 percentage points**. Mean token-level answer F1 was approximately **0.392 linked** versus **0.308 fulltext** on this selected sample, a *descriptive* difference not sufficient to establish general answer superiority, and the endpoints disagree. For 24 bridge examples, exact answers were **7/24 linked versus 6/24 fulltext**; for twelve comparisons, **3/12 linked versus 4/12 fulltext**. Very small subgroup sizes make strong generalization impossible.

Actual grounding remained fallible. A string-matching source quote check passed for **30/36 linked** and **31/36 fulltext** model answers; only **10/36** linked and **9/36** fulltext results were *both* exact answer correct and quoted an exact snippet. Even when *both gold articles* were included, direct answer outcomes were not assured: only **7 of those 15** linked-context examples and **6 of 12** fulltext-context examples matched the gold answer. Conversely, four fulltext and three linked exact answers occurred while a required source article was absent, which may indicate adequate alternative evidence, a one-document shortcut or model training knowledge; these are **not a verified proof chain**. A copied quote does not establish that the quoted text entails the generated answer. All outputs, full gold article/sentence overlap metrics and individual failure details remain in `source_grounded_model_qa_results_20261025.json` and the original JSONL, never silently repaired.

Concrete positive and negative examples: adding the original article **Herculaneum** to **Louis XVI style** changed a previously unsupported/unknown model answer into the human gold **“79 AD”**. In another case, replacing a wrong second article with **Shaka** enabled the correct gold **“1787”** from the original source chain. But a problematic article-title relationship changed a correctly answered **Oliver Parker** film-director question into the wrong **Peter Howitt** answer when the linked candidate was **“Johnny English”**, not the required **“Johnny English (film series)”** page. This is a direct **referent/title alias error** that a reliable identity-typed semantic system should reject, not additional factual knowledge. In a comparison case, selecting an incidental linked article caused the answer to change from the correct *Second Battle of Bull Run* to the wrong *Battle of Cold Harbor*. Case examples are **post-hoc**, not cherry-picked replacement scores, and exact original source titles and human gold are retained in the scored JSON.

There was also a strict **sham/control**: for twelve selected questions, both ranking methods supplied **byte-for-byte identical two source documents and excerpts** under the same prompt. **Eleven of twelve answers** and **nine of twelve citation strings** were identical. In the others, the local inference runtime generated slightly different responses despite temperature zero. Thus some model-output variability is **not caused by title-link intervention**. The observed exact-answer tie of 10/36 cannot justify ranking architectures, and even nonzero gains on a much larger sample would require an implementation that checks premise/quotation semantics rather than simply whether the response contains a source string.

**Answer to the original owner's open question, from newly measured results:** Cross-document **referent/source link retrieval genuinely improved access to both required facts at scale**, with strong task-type heterogeneity and a large nonliteral reference gap. **However, the modest model's actual downstream answer correctness did not improve on this fixed diagnostic sample.** This is a failure of the simplistic assumption that better evidence retrieval automatically yields new knowledge. The experiment *does not extract any KMD production DRS*, nor does a Hotpot question constitute an independently novel scientific discovery or an empirical falsification. It motivates an end-to-end gold-labeled **identity- and scope-preserving** parser test against the now measured source-neighbor baseline, including nonliteral aliases, quote/dream modes, time and negation, while reporting answer grounding separately from document recall. Such a production-grade DRS comparison remains outstanding.
